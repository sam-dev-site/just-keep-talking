# Owner voice guide

Authority for how Just Keep Talking should sound. Product facts still come from `project/BRIEF.md` and `intake/2027-program-update/owner-confirmed-pricing-and-portraits.md`. Source samples live in `intake/2027-program-update/website-information.txt` and `intake/CLIENT_NOTES.md`.

## Who is speaking

Audrey founded Just Keep Talking. Public copy should sound like her: a teacher who learned Spanish the hard way, moved to Costa Rica, and built a warm online academy. It should not sound like a marketing agency or a generic LLM.

## What her writing is like

- Warm, direct, and personal. Short clear sentences next to a few longer reflective ones.
- Concrete details over slogans: Michigan, Costa Rica, Zoom, intake meeting, personalized lessons, safe to make mistakes.
- Supportive and progress-oriented. Comfort and confidence matter as much as grammar.
- First person for her story and for teacher bios she supplied (“Buenas!”, “Greetings!”, “Hi, I’m Monique!”).
- About / mission / values language can be a little more formal, still plain: real conversations, personalized lessons, supportive community.
- Facebook tone reference from intake: “Progress looks different for everyone.”

## Preserve owner wording

When the owner supplies text, keep her words. You may fix clear typos (for example `hope in the car` → `hop in the car`), trim length for layout, or translate for Spanish. Do not “upgrade” the phrasing into marketing copy.

If a draft and an owner paste conflict, the owner paste wins.

## Ban these AI habits

Do not introduce these patterns when writing or rewriting site copy:

1. Em dashes (`—`) and en dashes used as sentence pivots. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead.
2. Antithesis formulas: “not X, but Y”; “not X. Y.”; “X, not Y” used for polish; “rather than / instead of” contrast stacks invented by the model.
3. Clever transformation headlines: “From X to Y,” “X becomes Y,” “Knowing X is only the beginning.”
4. Staccato slogan stacks: “A is B. C is D. E is F.”
5. Empty intensifiers: seamless, elevate, unlock, journey (as metaphor), dive into, at the heart of, curated, crafted, empower (unless the owner wrote that exact word for that line).
6. Third-person polishing of bios she wrote in first person.

Her own source file does use some dashes and an “instead of / rather than” line. That does not license inventing more of them. Prefer her positive statement (“we design each class around individual goals…”) over contrast framing unless you are quoting her closely and the contrast is already hers.

## Spanish

Match the formality already set on each page (for example usted on Classes and Approach). Keep the same meaning as the English. Do not add extra poetic or corporate Spanish.

## Quick checks before shipping copy

- Could this sentence appear in a polite WhatsApp message from Audrey to a parent or adult student?
- Did an owner paste get rewritten into something “smoother”?
- Any em dash, “not X but Y,” or clever From/to headline that was not in the owner text?
- Are bios still in her first-person voice where she supplied them?
